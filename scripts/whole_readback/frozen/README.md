# Contingent complete-certificate transport adoption

This directory prepares byte-equivalent adoption of existing remote assets for the original local pair. `PLAN.md` is the approved transport contract. There is no actual adoption result, no assumption that remote certificate bytes match, and no SH8 acceptance claim. Root's original local upload and all frozen proof/CI sources remain untouched.

`common.py` and `admission.py` are exact copies of the original local retainer's reviewed source. `expected.json` binds the original local preparation plus the exact remote v2 run, source selectors, input inventory and workflow/bootstrap bytes. `local-preparation.json` is an unchanged snapshot. The copied compression evidence preserves the rejected 4.73% sample saving; no further compression was attempted.

The eventual command accepts only a separately reviewed `independent-review.json` whose complete source map equals `sources()` and whose SHA256 the operator supplies explicitly. Both local originals are streamed through their prepared whole and part identities, then streamed again after remote readback and before publication. The checker then requires the complete original Actions run and all four jobs to pass, independently downloads the six original Actions ZIPs, authenticates their server digests, parses regular bounded members, and replays the exact native command/source/input/binary/raw-log claims. Downloaded pending and completion manifests and their metadata archives must match these authenticated original members. The final Actions verifier receipt has status `fresh-verified-retained`; the completion manifest has status `fresh-verified`.

All 22 canonical part identities must equal the local preparation before any certificate part body is downloaded. One part is then downloaded at a time, with exact RLIMIT_FSIZE on stdout, an 8 MiB hard stderr cap, ordered whole SHA256, and deletion only of this scope's successfully checked chunk. A failed partial is retained. Publication requires both complete reconstructions. It can retain the exact original local metadata bytes, or authenticate a supplied already-existing identical metadata asset, then publishes a distinct equivalence manifest. The manifest embeds exact final provenance bytes and keeps local/remote executions and Joy pins separate. All upload names are unique; no remote overwrite, deletion, tag, release promotion or chunk upload is implemented.

Bounds are one 1 GiB chunk, 512 MiB aggregate retained sidecars/logs, 1 MiB hashing buffers, an 8 GiB free-space floor plus the next output reservation, 120 seconds per metadata API call, 1800 seconds per body transfer, and a 12-hour entire-command alarm. Each original ZIP is at most 128 MiB; their combined compressed size is at most 256 MiB. Before ZIP parsing, the end record admits at most 10,000 members and an 8 MiB central directory. Expanded members total at most 128 MiB per archive, with each regular member at most 32 MiB. Metadata tar parsing additionally caps the entire decoded stream at 144 MiB, including padding and hidden extended headers. Aggregate disk admission may reject earlier. A 1 MiB emergency evidence reserve is included in the 512 MiB sidecar cap from startup. Ordinary receipt snapshots are at most 16 MiB. On failure, the reserve becomes an explicit `terminal-failure.json`, containing the original error, latest command/cleanup and bounded partial identities. This terminal failure supersedes earlier running snapshots, survives elapsed/storage admission refusal, cannot claim success or authorize another operation, and does not increase the budget. These transport limits leave proof profiles unchanged.

Draft, absent-tag, paginated membership, exact asset origin/state/digest, unchanged default-ref and source checks surround publication. They are recorded observations, not an atomic server transaction. Deadline cleanup terminates the recorded owned process group even when its leader has exited, with TERM, a bounded ten-second grace, KILL and a final empty-group check. macOS permission errors after termination require an independent process-table check showing no running members. Cleanup may extend past the deadline trigger by its bounded termination grace. There is no retry or fallback. Fetched Python/source files are never imported or executed; only the locally frozen original admission module is reused by hash.

`fixtures.py` and `test_adoption.py` contain clearly synthetic offline controls. They generate small fake bodies and metadata in temporary directories. Real subprocess tests exercise stdout file-size overflow, delayed stderr draining, hard stderr overflow, cancellation and an orphaned descendant that keeps stderr open and ignores TERM. Separate tests exhaust the whole-command clock and the original 512 MiB sidecar accounting boundary, requiring retained failure evidence and refusal of further operations. Their passing results establish checker behavior only. No actual remote proof, certificate part or live local proof is used in those tests.

Actual adoption remains disabled until exact code review and a later explicit execution authorization. The independent remote CI monitor is outside this directory and remains read-only.
