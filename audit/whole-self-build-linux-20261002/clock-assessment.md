# Physical-clock assessment after the original Linux failure

Status: assessment only. The original attempt remains failed under its immutable 7,200-second profile. No source optimization, changed bound or retry is represented as a result here.

The measured maximum directory sizes were 6,522,480,951 bytes at 7,199.171062273 seconds for C1 and 6,373,646,574 bytes at 7,199.400465589 seconds for C2. The observed averages are approximately 906,004 and 885,302 encoded bytes per second. If the complete native Linux streams had exactly the parent-reported local certificate lengths (11,977,015,727 and 10,569,174,820 bytes), and the remaining encoded-byte throughput stayed equal to those averages, production would take approximately 220.33 and 198.97 minutes. Both conditions are assumptions. Encoded size is not a semantic-work fraction, later computation may have different cost per byte, and no Linux complete size or fresh-verifier duration has been measured.

The original native build took 89.66/91.06 seconds; total bootstrap-to-production preparation took 200.94/197.28 seconds. Build time does not explain the two-hour cancellation. CPU, compression and Hemera costs were not separately profiled, so this evidence does not justify selecting a runtime optimization.

The original workflow assigns 300 minutes to build plus prove plus verify, 45 minutes to checked retention and 10 minutes to failure/success metadata; the job ceiling is 360 minutes. [GitHub's hosted-runner limit](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/limits), checked 2026-10-02, is six hours per job. Even if projected production plus verification fit the main step, allowing a four-hour producer and a two-hour verifier plus setup/retention exceeds that ceiling. The known Joy pin also rejects `time_ms` above 7,200,000; a CI-only clock selector cannot solve this failure.

A separate reviewed profile can preserve every exact compiler/JOB byte and logical, storage, RSS and proof limit while using a new Joy source pin whose explicit compacting host ceiling permits four hours. The original frozen input package, its 7,200-second historical profile, and these failure receipts must remain untouched. New execution clock bounds must be recorded as a separate profile, with the original workload identities and the changed Joy pin both explicit.

A bounded two-job design per generation is suitable for review:

1. A producer job builds the new committed native Joy pin, admits the immutable input asset, reconstructs the exact JOB, and runs the complete producer. A four-hour internal deadline and a slightly larger outer deadline apply only to this job. The original logical/proof/RSS/storage bounds remain unchanged. Capture success and the exact produced semantic coordinates are checked before staging, but completion remains pending independent verification.
2. Before the producer runner expires, that job transfers the complete certificate in uniquely named, individually hashed chunks to the existing unpublished evidence draft. A manifest binds repository/run/attempt/generation, source pins, profile, compiler/JOB, full certificate length/hash, ordered chunk identities and server asset IDs. Its status explicitly says pending fresh verification. No verified-completion claim or promotion occurs. Per-part readback and reconstructed whole digest remain required, with direct draft/tag checks before every upload. The transfer must have its own bounded phase and leave failure metadata time within six hours.
3. A separate fresh-verifier job accepts only the exact pending manifest from its producer dependency and fixed repository/run/attempt. It obtains the same reviewed native source pin, downloads and authenticates all chunks under the unchanged storage/free-space bounds, and verifies in a fresh process with empty PATH, expected compiler and JOB, and the original two-hour verification clock. It compares accepted semantic coordinates, complete compiler response and exact C2/C3 output bytes. Only then may it create a distinct verified-completion receipt referring to the immutable pending manifest and every exact downloaded chunk.

Producer staging cannot rely on the current success-only retention routine unchanged: that routine requires a fresh-verified producer report. The pending transport format and final verification receipt must be separate, and absence of the latter must always mean incomplete evidence. A producer's small Actions artifact can hand off the pending manifest and its digest; the large certificate stays in draft chunks. No existing assets should be overwritten or renamed. Duplicate names, unexpected asset origins, changed draft/tag state, missing chunks, mismatched digest, failed verification or interrupted transfer must fail closed with retained raw evidence.

A possible phase envelope is 45 minutes for producer setup/build, 245 minutes for bounded production, 45 minutes for pending transfer and 10 minutes for metadata, leaving 15 minutes within the six-hour ceiling for workflow overhead. These are proposed ceilings, not observed completion guarantees. The verifier gets its own setup/download, two-hour verification, completion-receipt and metadata phases. Transfer duration is currently unmeasured; conservative per-phase limits and the job ceiling must remain explicit. The first original Linux run provides no server upload-throughput measurement because retention was skipped.

This design increases scheduling room without changing the proof statement. It still requires review of the Joy host-ceiling change, its committed pin, exact CI source/selector changes, pending transport, download admission and the final completion contract before dispatch.
